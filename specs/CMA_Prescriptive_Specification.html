CMA prescriptive specification

Residential Projects
Birmingham, Alabama
Plan-Set Sale Edition · 1.0

These notes accompany a set of construction drawings prepared by CMA and conveyed to the purchaser for use on a single residence. CMA provides design documents only; CMA does not administer or observe the construction. The purchaser, owner, and the licensed builder of record are wholly responsible for execution, code compliance, means and methods, and the safety and adequacy of the work. These notes establish the minimum standards CMA requires of any home built from its drawings.

01General Conditions & Scope of Drawings

Documents conveyed. The drawings, schedules, and these notes together constitute CMA's design documents. They convey design intent and prescribe minimum finish, material, and assembly standards. They are not a contract between CMA and any builder. CMA is not a party to the construction contract.

No construction administration. CMA will not perform site visits, submittal review, RFI response, change-order review, punch lists, or any other construction-administration service unless engaged separately under a written supplemental agreement. Absent such an agreement, no advice, comment, or silence by CMA shall be construed as approval of any work.

Use and copyright. The drawings are licensed for the construction of one residence at the address indicated. Reuse, adaptation, or further distribution is prohibited without written authorization. Removing CMA's seal or title block voids the license.

02Drawing Interpretation & Precedence

Order of precedence. Where conflict exists among the documents, the more stringent requirement applies. Among themselves, the documents are read in this order: written addenda or revisions; large-scale details; small-scale details and elevations; plans; schedules; these notes. Written dimensions govern over scaled measurements. Do not scale drawings.

Coverage. Items shown on one drawing and not another are required as if shown on all. Mechanical, electrical, and plumbing layouts are diagrammatic; routing, sizing, and final coordination are the builder's responsibility.

Discrepancies. The builder shall notify the owner in writing of any conflict, omission, or ambiguity before proceeding. Work installed in conflict with the documents is at the builder's risk.

03Site Documentation & Existing Conditions

Field verification. All existing conditions, dimensions, elevations, setbacks, easements, and utility locations shall be verified by the builder before excavation, fabrication, or ordering. The drawings rely on information furnished to CMA at the time of issue and are not a survey.

Survey, soils, & engineering. A current boundary and topographic survey, a geotechnical report, and structural, mechanical, and civil engineering stamped by Alabama-licensed engineers are required where conditions warrant. None of these are provided by CMA unless specifically noted on the cover sheet.

Hidden conditions. Discovery of hidden conditions during construction does not authorize departure from the design intent without written consultation with CMA, which may be obtained for an additional fee.

04Contractor Qualifications & Licensing

Builder of record. The work shall be performed by a builder properly licensed by the State of Alabama Home Builders Licensure Board (Alabama Code §34-14A) and registered in the project's jurisdiction. The builder shall carry general liability and builder's-risk insurance and shall name the owner as an additional insured.

Specialized trades. Mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and gas-fitting work shall be performed by trades licensed by the Alabama Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board, the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board, and the Alabama Board of HVAC Contractors as applicable. Roofing, masonry, and stucco shall be performed by tradesmen with a minimum of five years' experience in custom residential work of comparable detail. Documentation of licensure and insurance shall be retained on site.

05Submittals & Approvals

No CMA review. Under this edition, CMA does not receive, review, stamp, or approve submittals, shop drawings, samples, or product data. The builder and owner shall jointly review and approve all such items before fabrication or order.

Recommended pre-fabrication review. CMA strongly recommends that the owner retain CMA, or another licensed architect, to review shop drawings for cabinetry, exterior millwork, windows, doors, and roof and floor framing before fabrication. Failure to do so does not relieve the builder of responsibility for conformance with the design.

06Material Standards & Substitutions

Standard quality. All materials shall be new, unused, of first quality, and free of defects. No reclaimed, salvaged, or seconds material unless expressly indicated on the drawings.

CMA standing requirements. Half-round copper gutters and round copper downspouts. Copper flashing at all roof transitions, valleys, drip edges, pitch breaks, chimneys, beam caps, and roof-to-wall conditions. Copper or lead-coated copper pans at all through-wall and shower conditions. Stainless steel or silicon-bronze fasteners at all exterior wood, brick mold, porches, decks, rafter tails, and any concealed fastening in contact with treated lumber. No exposed galvanized fasteners on visible exterior surfaces.

Wood. Exterior trim, rafter tails, fascia, beadboard, and porch ceilings shall be a clear-grade rot-resistant species (Western Red Cedar, Spanish Cedar, Cypress, or Sapele). Finger-jointed material is not permitted on the exterior. Interior trim and millwork shall be solid wood — paint-grade poplar or stain-grade species per the drawings.

Substitutions. Items shown on the drawings or named in these notes are required. Any substitution is the owner's and builder's decision and shall be documented in writing between them. CMA does not warrant the equivalence of any substituted product, and substitution does not transfer any design liability to CMA.

07Workmanship Standards

All work shall be performed by tradesmen skilled and experienced in the work of their trade, in a manner consistent with the highest standard of residential custom construction. Lines shall be true, surfaces plumb and level, joints tight and consistent, reveals uniform. Trim returns, miters, and scribes shall be hand-fit; caulk shall not be used to fill gaps in trim. Exposed fastener heads shall be set, filled, and finished. Drywall shall be Level 4 minimum, Level 5 in raking light and gloss-paint conditions. Paint shall not bridge across separate trim members. Flashing, weather barrier, and roofing assemblies shall be installed to manufacturers' published instructions; deviation voids warranty and is not authorized.

08Change Orders & Field Modifications

No change to the design — including, without limitation, changes to plan, elevation, roof form, opening sizes, exterior material, exterior color, principal interior finish, or structural assembly — shall be made without written authorization from the owner. Field modifications that depart from the drawings are at the builder's and owner's sole risk. CMA's design liability does not extend to work as modified in the field without CMA's written consultation.

09Quality Assurance & Final Inspection

Owner-led inspections. The owner is encouraged to conduct, directly or through a qualified third-party inspector, a pre-pour foundation review, a pre-drywall framing/MEP/flashing review, and a substantial-completion punch walk before final payment. CMA may be retained for these reviews under separate agreement.

Concealed work. Framing, mechanical, electrical, plumbing, waterproofing, and flashing shall be photographed before being concealed. Photographs shall be retained by the owner for the duration of any applicable warranty.

Limitation. Any review by CMA, if performed, is limited to general conformance with the design intent and does not relieve the builder of sole responsibility for means, methods, sequencing, safety, or completeness of the work.

10Code Compliance — Alabama Residential

All work shall comply with all codes, standards, and regulations in force in the project's jurisdiction at the time of permit, including without limitation the IRC, IECC, NEC, IPC, and IMC as adopted by the State of Alabama and the local authority having jurisdiction, and any local zoning, historic, or overlay-district requirements. Where this specification is more stringent than code, this specification governs. Code compliance is solely the builder's responsibility.

CMA prescriptive specification

Residential Projects
Birmingham, Alabama
CMA Process Edition · 1.0

This specification governs any residence designed by CMA and built under CMA's construction administration. It is incorporated by reference into the construction drawings and into the owner-builder agreement. Where this specification, the drawings, and the schedules conflict, the more stringent requirement applies.

01General Conditions & Scope of Drawings

Authority of documents. The construction drawings, schedules, and this specification together constitute the contract documents. CMA is the architect of record and will provide construction administration consistent with AIA B101 / B105 as modified by the owner-architect agreement.

Architect's role. CMA will conduct site visits at intervals appropriate to the stage of the work, review submittals, respond to RFIs, evaluate change proposals, certify payment, and conduct substantial- and final-completion walks. CMA does not direct or control means, methods, sequencing, or safety, and is not responsible for the contractor's failure to perform the work in accordance with the contract documents.

02Drawing Interpretation & Precedence

Order of precedence. Where conflict exists, the more stringent requirement applies. Among themselves, the documents are read in this order: signed change orders; addenda; large-scale details; small-scale details and elevations; plans; schedules; this specification. Written dimensions govern over scaled measurements. Do not scale drawings.

Coverage. Items shown on one drawing and not another are required as if shown on all. MEP layouts are diagrammatic; the contractor is responsible for coordinated routing that respects framing, finishes, and ceiling heights shown.

Discrepancies. The contractor shall notify the architect in writing of any conflict, omission, or ambiguity before proceeding. Work installed in conflict with the documents will be removed and replaced at the contractor's expense.

03Site Documentation & Existing Conditions

Field verification. The contractor shall verify all existing conditions, dimensions, elevations, setbacks, easements, and utility locations before excavation, fabrication, or ordering, and shall promptly notify the architect in writing of any discrepancy.

Records on site. The contractor shall maintain on site at all times a current set of stamped drawings, this specification, the project schedule, the submittal log, and the RFI log. The contractor shall photograph all framing, MEP, waterproofing, and flashing work before it is concealed and transmit those photographs to the architect weekly.

As-builts. Deviations shall be redlined on a dedicated record set and delivered to the architect at substantial completion.

04Contractor Qualifications & Licensing

Contractor. The contractor shall be properly licensed by the State of Alabama Home Builders Licensure Board (Alabama Code §34-14A), registered in the project's jurisdiction, and shall employ a full-time on-site superintendent with a minimum of five years' experience in custom residential work of comparable scale and detail. The contractor shall carry general liability and builder's-risk insurance naming the owner and architect as additional insured.

Specialized trades. Mechanical, electrical, plumbing, and gas-fitting work shall be performed by trades licensed by the Alabama Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board, the Alabama Electrical Contractors Board, and the Alabama Board of HVAC Contractors as applicable. Subcontractors for masonry, roofing, plaster/stucco, millwork, and finish carpentry shall be similarly qualified and approved by the architect before commencement.

05Submittals & Approval Timelines

Required submittals. Prior to fabrication or order, the contractor shall submit, for the architect's review and stamp: shop drawings for cabinetry, millwork, stairs, railings, exterior trim assemblies, doors, windows, roof and floor framing, and structural steel; physical samples of all exterior materials, including masonry, mortar, stucco, roofing, siding, paint, and stain; and product data for any item where a model, finish, or color must be selected.

Turnaround. The architect shall be allowed ten working days for first review and five working days for re-submittal review. The contractor shall plan the schedule accordingly. Fabrication or ordering before written approval is at the contractor's sole risk.

Submittal log. The contractor shall maintain a running log with dates, status, and revision numbers, and shall transmit the log to the architect with each pay application.

06Material Standards & Substitution Policy

Standard quality. All materials shall be new, unused, of first quality, and free of defects.

CMA standing requirements. Half-round copper gutters and round copper downspouts. Copper flashing at all roof transitions, valleys, drip edges, pitch breaks, chimneys, beam caps, and roof-to-wall conditions. Copper or lead-coated copper pans at through-wall and shower conditions. Stainless steel or silicon-bronze fasteners at all exterior wood, brick mold, porches, decks, rafter tails, and any concealed fastening in contact with treated lumber. No exposed galvanized fasteners on visible exterior surfaces.

Wood. Exterior trim, rafter tails, fascia, beadboard, and porch ceilings shall be a clear-grade rot-resistant species (Western Red Cedar, Spanish Cedar, Cypress, or Sapele); finger-jointed material is not permitted on the exterior. Interior trim and millwork shall be solid wood per the drawings.

Substitutions. No substitution shall be made without the architect's prior written approval. Substitution requests shall include physical samples, full technical data, and a written statement that the substitution equals or exceeds the specified item in performance, durability, and appearance. The architect's review of a substitution does not waive any other requirement of the contract documents.

07Workmanship Standards

All work shall be performed by tradesmen skilled and experienced in the work of their trade, in a manner consistent with the highest standard of residential custom construction. Lines shall be true, surfaces plumb and level, joints tight and consistent, reveals uniform. Trim returns, miters, and scribes shall be hand-fit; caulk shall not be used to fill gaps in trim. Exposed fastener heads shall be set, filled, and finished. Drywall shall be Level 4 minimum, Level 5 in raking light and gloss-paint conditions. Paint shall not bridge across separate trim members. The architect may, at any time, reject work that does not conform to the contract documents — including work shown on previously stamped shop drawings; such rejected work shall be removed and replaced at no cost to the owner.

08Change Order Protocol

No change to the scope, quantity, material, finish, or method of any work shall be executed without a written change order signed by the owner and reviewed by the architect. Field directives from the owner do not constitute change orders; the contractor shall reduce any verbal request to a written proposal stating cost, schedule impact, and a single combined total, and shall not proceed until signed. Work performed without a signed change order is performed at the contractor's risk and expense. Substitutions of any kind require a change order.

09Quality Assurance & Final Inspection

Required milestone reviews. A pre-pour foundation review, a pre-drywall walk-through (framing, MEP rough-in, waterproofing, flashing, blocking), and an outlet/switch walk-through with the owner — held after framing and before any wall finishes — are required and shall be scheduled with the architect with five working days' notice.

Substantial & final completion. The architect will conduct a substantial-completion walk and issue a punch list. All punch items shall be closed and re-verified before final payment. A one-year warranty walk shall be scheduled by the contractor at eleven months following substantial completion.

Limitation. The architect's review, on site or through submittals, is for general conformance with the design intent only and does not relieve the contractor of sole responsibility for means, methods, sequencing, safety, or for the correctness and completeness of the work.

10Code Compliance — Alabama Residential

All work shall comply with all codes, standards, and regulations in force in the project's jurisdiction at the time of permit, including without limitation the IRC, IECC, NEC, IPC, and IMC as adopted by the State of Alabama and the local authority having jurisdiction, and any local zoning, historic, or overlay-district requirements. Where this specification is more stringent than code, this specification governs. The contractor is responsible for code compliance and for obtaining all required inspections and certificates.
